    The whole story, chapter by chapter

      01

      AI football commentary that shows its working

      watches a football match and commentates on it, like a broadcaster would. The difference: before any line goes on air, a second AI checks every fact in it against what actually happened on the pitch. If a claim can't be proven, it doesn't air.

      What it does

      As a match plays, one AI spots the moments that matter: a goal, a big chance, a team taking control. Another writes the line a commentator would say. A third, the Critic, checks the line against the match's raw events. It recounts the passes, re-measures the distances, checks that every event cited had actually happened yet. Only then does the line reach the screen, with its evidence attached and a receipt showing what it cost.

      Why I built it

      AI writes fluent sentences, and fluent isn't the same as true. In sport, a wrong number on air costs the broadcaster the viewer's trust. I wanted commentary you can audit: every claim traceable to the moment it describes, and a system honest enough to say "I can't prove that" instead of making it up. If a model fails mid-match, the broadcast keeps going and says so.

      02

      AI can talk about football. It can't prove what it says.

      Language models write fluent commentary in seconds. They also invent numbers, mix up minutes and cite moments that haven't happened yet. On a live broadcast, a wrong line reaches every viewer before anyone can check it.

      Confident and unchecked

      A model sounds just as sure when it's wrong. Without a check between the draft and the screen, every error goes out live.

      One feed for every fan

      An analyst wants expected goals and pressing patterns. A casual viewer wants to know why the crowd just roared. Most feeds serve one and lose the other.

      English first, everyone else later

      The game is watched around the world, but live commentary is mostly written in English. Urdu and Arabic speakers usually get a translation, not commentary written for them.

      When the model stalls, the feed goes dark

      A live system that waits on one slow or failed model call goes quiet at exactly the moment the match needs it.

      04

      How a claim gets on air

      Every event takes the hot path: fast, deterministic, no language model. Each alert then takes the warm path, where one agent writes, another checks, and nothing airs until it passes.

      One claim, followed all the way

      The claim from the front page, through each step of synthetic match seed 7.

      1. Spot

        The Scout reads every event as it arrives and flags goals, big chances and swings in momentum.

      2. Write

        The Narrator drafts the line for air and cites the event behind every fact in it.

      3. Check

        The Critic recomputes each number from the raw events, with the same formulas the Scout uses. A wrong number, a missing citation or an event from the future sends the claim back, up to three attempts.

      4. Air

        The Localizer turns the verified claim into an on-screen cue for casual fans or analysts. A claim that never passes never airs.

      05

      When an agent fails, the broadcast keeps going

      The Orchestrator supervises the line. If the Narrator or Critic stops responding, it retries once. If the agent is still down, viewers get detection-only cues, marked as unverified, and the broadcast says when full service is back.

      1. Full serviceEvery cue verified by the Critic
      2. Detection onlyCues keep airing, marked unverified
      3. RecoveredAnnounced on air, verification resumes

      A test knocks the Narrator out four times in a row mid-match and checks that the cues never stop.

      06

      Synthetic by design

      Every match is generated from a seed: invented clubs, invented players and realistic volumes, about a thousand passes, 29 shots and three goals a match on average. The same seed always replays the same match, so every claim on this site can be reproduced.

      Replay this match yourself:

      python -m court run --seed 7
      07

      Trust is the product

      A model can already write commentary. The open question is whether anyone can rely on it, live, with millions watching. is built around that question.

      “We will see whether you have told the truth.”

      King Solomon to the hoopoe, Surah An-Naml 27:27. Why that line is the name

      1. Nothing unverified goes to air

        Broadcasters answer for every word on screen. Each claim carries its own audit trail: the events behind it, the check it passed and the attempt it took.

      2. The model writes, the evidence decides

        We don't ask the model to be careful. We check its work with the same formulas the Scout uses, and refuse anything that doesn't add up.

      3. Commentary in the fan's own language

        Urdu and Arabic are part of the plan from the start, at native register, and every string is reviewed by a native speaker before it ships.

      4. Failure is designed in

        Models will be slow, rate-limited or down mid-match. The system degrades, says so on air and recovers, instead of falling silent.

      5. Built in the open

        The goals are declared on this site before the deadline, and the code goes public with the entry. Judge the work against the promise.

      08

      What runs today, and what's next

      Built in the open, one tested commit at a time. Everything under “Live” runs today, on synthetic matches.

      Live

      1. Oct 18
        The studio, live on Azure

        Watch a synthetic match narrated live, with every cue's receipt and the Critic's refusals on screen. Take the narrator off air and watch the broadcast keep going. It runs on Azure Container Apps with its own identity and no keys, open through the judging period. Open the studio.

      2. Oct 18
        The models competed for the job

        gpt-5-mini and gpt-4.1-mini narrated the same 50 alerts from five unseen matches, judged by a rule fixed before the run. Both were grounded every time, with no invented numbers. gpt-4.1-mini put a cue on air in 1.3 seconds against 3.7, at a third of the cost, so it writes the commentary now.

      3. Oct 18
        A receipt on every cue

        Every line on air carries its receipt: who wrote it, how many numbers the Critic checked, how many facts it recomputed from the match, the events cited, the time from alert to air, and the tokens and cost.

      4. Oct 18
        The control room

        Every agent's work traced into Application Insights, with keys off: each alert's outcome and the Critic's reasons, every model call with its tokens. A workbook charts it, and the demo video walks through it.

      5. Oct 18
        Every output kept

        Each match's cues, the claims the Critic refused with its reasons, and the model cost go to an audit trail on Azure Cosmos DB's free tier. The writes run in the background, so a slow store can't delay a cue.

      6. Oct 18
        Commentary written on Microsoft Foundry

        gpt-4.1-mini writes each line from a fact sheet that code prepared, and gpt-5-mini takes over if it fails. There are no keys: access is through Microsoft Entra ID only.

      7. Oct 18
        The Critic checks every number

        If a line states a number that code didn't compute, the Critic refuses it and sends it back to the model with its reasons.

      8. Oct 18
        Momentum calls on air

        A momentum claim must cite at least three of the side's own shots or passes into the final third, all from inside the spell it describes.

      9. Oct 18
        Recovery, proven by a real fault

        When model access failed during a test run, the broadcast switched to detection-only cues and said so on air, instead of going silent.

      10. Oct 18
        Momentum measured against the truth

        Each synthetic match hides the real momentum swings. The Scout makes few, sure calls: on matches never used for tuning, 62% of its calls are right, and it catches about a quarter of the swings.

      11. Oct 17
        A synthetic match generator

        Invented clubs and players, real-match volumes, calibrated over 100 seeds. The same seed gives the same match every time.

      09

      Onwards & upwards

      The hackathon is stage one. keeps going after it, toward an AI fourth official that sits beside the referee team.

      The north star: the fourth official

      An official that watches every moment and judges calls in a blink, at the strictness the competition chooses. It shows the evidence for every decision and explains it to the referee, the broadcast and the crowd, each in their own language. It advises. The human referee decides.

      1. In progress

        The court of claims

        The hackathon, October 2026

        Commentary that proves every claim. Five agents, a Critic that refuses what it can't verify, and a broadcast that survives failure.

        Bridge: a “Ref's call” cue, if time allows. The Scout flags a foul, the Narrator states the likely decision, the Critic checks it at a chosen strictness.

      2. Next

        The decision explainer

        Late 2026 into 2027

        When the referee makes a call, explains it to the crowd and the viewers, with the evidence and the Law it rests on, in their language.

        Gate: agreement with ground-truth decisions measured over many seeds, and explanations signed off by a referee panel.

      3. Planned

        Perception

        2027

        The Scout gets eyes: fouls classified from multiple camera angles and from player tracking, with every prediction citing its frames.

        Gate: matching published results on a public benchmark, with evidence attached to every call.

      4. North star

        The fourth official

        Beyond

        Real-time advice to the referee team, faster than a video review takes today. Flashpoint predictions, a published strictness spectrum, and announcements the whole stadium understands.

        Path: simulation and esports first, then grassroots football, then professional trials inside the game's own rules.

      What holds at every stage

      • Evidence before verdict. No evidence, no call.
      • The human decides. advises and explains, never overrules.
      • Strictness is a setting. The same incident gets the same call.
      • The Laws come first. Encoded as rules where they can be; judgment is flagged as judgment.
      • Explain to everyone. Referee, broadcast and crowd, each at their own depth.
      • Never go dark. Degrade, announce, recover.

      hudhud, the hoopoe, in Urdu, Persian and Arabic

      Named for the messenger who came back with proof

      is the hoopoe's name. The bird carries news through some of the oldest stories in those languages, and in the best known, no one acts on its news until it has been checked.

      Sure news

      The Quran, Surah An-Naml 27:20–28

      King Solomon notices the hoopoe is missing and demands a clear reason for its absence. It returns from Sheba with news it calls certain. Solomon doesn't take its word for it.

      بِنَبَإٍ يَقِينٍ

      “With sure news.” 27:22

      سَنَنظُرُ أَصَدَقْتَ أَمْ كُنتَ مِنَ الْكَاذِبِينَ

      “We will see whether you have told the truth, or are one of the liars.” 27:27

      He tests the report before he acts on it. That is the Critic's job: no claim reaches the screen until it has been checked against the match events. The hoopoe reported to a court, and so do the agents, which is why the code is called court.

      The guide

      Farid ud-Din Attar, The Conference of the Birds, 12th century

      In Attar's Persian poem the hoopoe, once Solomon's messenger, leads the birds of the world in search of the Simurgh, their king. Thirty survive the journey and discover that si murgh means “thirty birds”: together, they are what they were looking for. The agents work the same way. None of them sees the whole match, together they do, and the Orchestrator leads.

      Heads-up display

      Broadcast English

      A HUD is a heads-up display, the layer of graphics over a live picture. That's what makes: verified cues, timed to the match and ready to overlay.

      Five feathers

      The mark

      The hoopoe's crest fans open like a broadcast signal, and the mark gives it one feather per agent. The name says itself twice, hud-hud, two beats like a chant from the stands. Written short, it's : the display, squared.

      Every culture has a messenger that brings back the truth. Odin's ravens, Huginn and Muninn, fly over the world each day and report what they saw. Noah's dove returns with an olive leaf, proof in its beak that the flood is going down. Hermes carries the Greek gods' messages on winged sandals. In Urdu, Persian and Arabic, the messenger is the hudhud.
