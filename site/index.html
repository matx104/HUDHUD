How a claim gets on air

  1. Spot

    The Scout reads every event as it arrives and flags goals, big chances and swings in momentum.

  2. Write

    The Narrator drafts the line for air and cites the event behind every fact in it.

  3. Check

    The Critic recomputes each number from the raw events, with the same formulas the Scout uses. A wrong number, a missing citation or an event from the future sends the claim back for a rewrite, up to three attempts.

  4. Air

    The Localizer turns the verified claim into an on-screen cue for casual fans or analysts. A claim that never passes never airs.

When an agent fails, the broadcast keeps going

The Orchestrator supervises the line. If the Narrator or Critic stops responding, it retries once. If the agent is still down, viewers get detection-only cues, marked as unverified, and the broadcast says when full service is back.

A test fails the Narrator four times in a row mid-match and checks that cues never stop.

  1. Full serviceEvery cue verified by the Critic
  2. Detection onlyCues keep airing, marked unverified
  3. RecoveredAnnounced on air, verification resumes

Synthetic by design

Every match is generated from a seed: invented clubs, invented players and realistic volumes, about a thousand passes, 29 shots and three goals a match on average. The same seed always replays the same match, so every claim on this page can be reproduced.

python -m court run --seed 7

hudhud, the hoopoe, in Urdu, Persian and Arabic

Named for the messenger who came back with proof

is the hoopoe's name. The bird carries news through some of the oldest stories in those languages, and in the best known, no one acts on its news until it has been checked.

Sure news

The Quran, Surah An-Naml 27:20–28

King Solomon notices the hoopoe is missing and demands a clear reason for its absence. It returns from Sheba with news it calls certain. Solomon doesn't take its word for it.

بِنَبَإٍ يَقِينٍ

“With sure news.” 27:22

سَنَنظُرُ أَصَدَقْتَ أَمْ كُنتَ مِنَ الْكَاذِبِينَ

“We will see whether you have told the truth, or are one of the liars.” 27:27

He tests the report before he acts on it. That is the Critic's job: no claim reaches the screen until it has been checked against the match events. The hoopoe reported to a court, and so do the agents, which is why the code is called court.

The guide

Farid ud-Din Attar, The Conference of the Birds, 12th century

In Attar's Persian poem the hoopoe, once Solomon's messenger, leads the birds of the world in search of the Simurgh, their king. Thirty survive the journey and discover that si murgh means “thirty birds”: together, they are what they were looking for. The agents work the same way. None of them sees the whole match, together they do, and the Orchestrator leads.

Five feathers

The mark

The hoopoe's crest fans open like a broadcast signal, and the mark gives it one feather per agent. The name says itself twice, hud-hud, two beats like a chant from the stands. Written short, it's : the display, squared.

Every culture has a messenger that brings back the truth. Odin's ravens, Huginn and Muninn, fly over the world each day and report what they saw. Noah's dove returns with an olive leaf, proof in its beak that the flood is going down. Hermes carries the Greek gods' messages on winged sandals. In Urdu, Persian and Arabic, the messenger is the hudhud.
