# The demo video: the script

> Length: about 3:30. The first 2:00 are the complete product story (the judges may stop there).
> Everything past 2:00 is proof, ambition, and the person — for anyone who keeps watching.
> Every shot answers one question the viewer is asking at that moment. Nothing is on screen
> without a reason to be there.

## Act 1 — The hook: AI that refuses to lie (0:00–0:20)

**What the viewer is thinking:** "Why should I care?"

**0:00 — the site (20 seconds)**
The viewer sees the HUDHUD home page. A claim is on screen: "What aired" vs "First draft."
The draft was refused — it cited an event from the future. The rewrite was verified.
This is the whole idea in one image: an AI that checks its own work and refuses to air
what it can't prove. The viewer scrolls down: the front three (HUD², Ask, the Ledger),
then the team sheet. The site IS the product.

> "AI can commentate a football match. And it sounds just as sure when it's wrong.
> HUDHUD is a broadcast that refuses to air a claim it can't prove.
> Every call comes with its evidence."

## Act 2 — The product working (0:20–2:00)

**What the viewer is thinking:** "OK, show me."

**0:20 — the studio, live on Azure (30 seconds)**
A match is playing. Lines are airing. Each carries its evidence and a receipt.
The Critic's desk shows what it refused and why.
The viewer watches the feed for 5 seconds — lines appearing, the match alive.
Then scrolls slowly through the studio: the feed, the Critic's desk (refused drafts,
struck through with the reasons), the receipts, the ledger panel growing its blocks.
This is the product in its element: working, live, on Azure.

> "This is my studio, live on Azure Container Apps. A model on Microsoft Foundry
> writes each line, from facts my code computed. And a Critic — a second agent —
> recomputes every number from the raw match events before anything airs.
> If a claim can't be proven, it goes back with the reasons, or it's spiked on screen."

**0:50 — HUD²: three lenses on one match (20 seconds)**
The same match through the referee's eyes: a foul in the box, the likely penalty
pinned where it happened. The viewer sees the call recomputed from Law 12.
Then the commentator's lens, then the fan's.
Same match, same facts, three ways to see it.

> "HUD² shows the same match through three lenses. The referee sees the foul,
> the likely call pinned where it happened, read from the Law and recomputed
> by the Critic. Commentators and fans get lenses of their own."

**1:10 — the broadcast that never dies (15 seconds)**
The narrator is taken off air. The viewer watches the broadcast keep going:
the Scout's own facts, cited, marked unverified. Then the recovery, announced on air.
This is the moment that separates a demo from a product.

> "What happens when the model fails? Watch. The broadcast drops to the Scout's
> own facts — cited, marked unverified — says so on air, and brings the
> commentary back when the model recovers. The feed never goes dark."

**1:25 — Urdu (12 seconds)**
The same verified line, in Urdu, on screen and spoken aloud.

> "The same law holds in Urdu: a translation may not add a number or change a name.
> On screen, and spoken."

**1:37 — Arabic (10 seconds)**
The same line, in Arabic, spoken.

> *(no narration — let the Arabic voice speak for itself)*

**1:47 — Ask the match (20 seconds)**
The viewer sees a question typed: "Who has completed the most passes?"
The answer arrives — checked, with its proof visible. Then a second question:
"What are the odds on Brackenfield?" The answer refuses to help with betting.
Both are sealed into the match's ledger.

> "Ask the match anything. Code counts every number, the Critic checks the answer,
> and it's sealed in the match's ledger, verified in your browser.
> And it never helps anyone bet."

**— 2:00 — the judges may stop here. They've seen the product work. —**

## Act 3 — The proof (2:00–3:00)

**What the viewer is thinking:** "Is this real?"

**2:07 — the control room on Azure (25 seconds)**
The Azure portal: the HUDHUD workbook. Every match the court has narrated,
what became of every alert, why the Critic refused, what each match cost.
The viewer scrolls through the workbook, section by section: the tiles,
the alert outcomes, the refusal reasons, the model costs, the latency.
This is the system's heartbeat, visible to anyone.

> "Five agents on Microsoft Agent Framework, models chosen by evaluation,
> every call traced. The control room on Azure tells the whole story:
> what became of every alert, why the Critic refused, what each match cost."

**2:32 — the ledger, verified in the browser (12 seconds)**
The match's history: 111 blocks chained by hashes. One click, and the viewer
watches every hash verify in their own browser. Every block turns green.

> *(no narration — the visual of the blocks turning green is the proof)*

## Act 4 — The ambition and the builder (3:00–3:30)

**What the viewer is thinking:** "Where does this go? Who made it?"

**2:44 — the goal (10 seconds)**
Six awards, one engineer, every lane built to win.

> "Six awards. One engineer. Every lane built to win."

**2:54 — the roadmap (15 seconds)**
The stairs rise: the court of claims (today), the decision explainer,
perception from cameras, the AI fourth official.

> "And this is stage one. The road goes on: the decision explainer, then perception
> from cameras, all the way to an AI fourth official beside the referee.
> It advises. The human decides."

**3:09 — the builder (12 seconds)**
The profile, the kit. One engineer, from Karachi.

> "Muhammad Abdullah Tariq. Principal Software Engineer, from Karachi.
> Every agent, every line, built alone."

**3:21 — the close (10 seconds)**
The wordmark. The promise. The address.

> "HUDHUD. Every call comes with its evidence. hudhud.com.pk."

## Total: about 3:30

The first 2:00 is a complete product story. The rest is proof, ambition, and the person.
