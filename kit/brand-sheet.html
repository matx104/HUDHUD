{{mark}} ہدہد The short form, for the icon and the monogram; and the name in Urdu, Persian and Arabic: hudhud, the hoopoe.

The mark

A hoopoe's crest of five feathers, one for each agent: Scout, Narrator, Critic, Localizer and Orchestrator — printed on the classic match ball in the club's colours: white hexagons, pitch-green pentagons, seen from above the pole so one pentagon sits at the bottom of the ball. The feathers root in that pentagon and fan up as one connected crest, every tip reaching the ball's rim, keylined in navy so they print onto the white; the Critic's feather stands at the centre, in gold, because nothing airs until it has checked it. A wide scope ring frames the badge, and a gold reticle locks on the root of the tail — the point where all five meet and every claim is checked. The agents' glyphs (eye, pen, check, globe, hub) appear only in the large lockup, never in the small mark.

The name

In Surah An-Naml, 27:20–28, the hoopoe returns to Solomon "with sure news", and Solomon checks it before acting on it. That check is the Critic.

Using it

On deep navy, the Critic's feather always gold. Never beside a league, club or broadcaster mark: every club and player in {{name}} is invented. The type is under the SIL Open Font License.
