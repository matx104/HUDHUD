AI football commentary that shows its working

{{name}} watches a football match and commentates on it, like a broadcaster would. The difference: before any line goes on air, a second AI checks every fact in it against what actually happened on the pitch. If a claim can't be proven, it doesn't air.

Five agents, one line to air

Scout

Reads every event and flags the goals, big chances, great passes and momentum swings. No model, under a millisecond.

Narrator

Drafts the line from facts the code computed, citing the event behind each one. A model on Microsoft Foundry.

Critic

Recomputes every number from the raw events. A wrong figure, a missing citation or an event from the future sends it back.

Localizer

Turns the checked claim into an on-screen cue in English, Urdu or Arabic, with its player tags.

On air

With its evidence attached and a receipt: who wrote it, what was checked, what it cost.

Orchestratorsupervises the whole line. If a model fails mid-match, the broadcast carries on with the Scout's own facts, says so on screen, and recovers.

Why I built it

AI writes fluent sentences, and fluent isn't the same as true. In sport, a wrong number on air costs the broadcaster the viewer's trust. I wanted commentary you can audit: every claim traceable to the moment it describes, and a system honest enough to say "I can't prove that" instead of making it up.

The hackathon

{{name}} is my entry to Inside the Game: Developer Hackathon, run by Microsoft with the Premier League from October 6 to 27, 2026: AI that turns football-realistic, synthetic match data into insight, commentary and recaps. Judging runs to November 10; winners are announced on November 20.
